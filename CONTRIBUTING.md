# Contributing guide

Entropic Science values all forms of contribution: code, research, writing, organizing, promoting, funding, and reviewing. You do not need to be a programmer to contribute meaningfully.

Please read the [Code of conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) before participating.

## Getting started

**Step 1: Join Discord** at [discord.gg/8ZrxF9qU](https://discord.gg/8ZrxF9qU) and introduce yourself in `#introductions`. Mention your background, interests, and what kind of work you would like to do.

**Step 2: Explore active projects.** Browse the repositories listed in the [README](README.md) and the task boards on [GitHub Projects](https://github.com/orgs/Entropic-Science/projects). Look for issues labeled `good-first-issue` for accessible starting points.

**Step 3: Join a weekly call** (`[PLACEHOLDER: day/time]`) to ask questions, request contributor access to repositories and project boards, and find collaborators.

**Step 4: Start contributing.** Pick any of the activities listed below. No formal approval is needed to begin. If your work involves code, submit a clearly articulated pull request to the relevant repository. If your work is non-code, share it in the `#contributions` channel on Discord.

## Contribution areas

Here is where we need help, roughly in order of current priority:

### Quantum-random systems infrastructure
Software development on quantum-random AI/LLM inference pipelines, QRNG hardware integration, gRPC server standards, PRNG/QRNG comparison modes, and alternative entropy sources (DRAM timing noise, etc.).

### Research and evaluation
Designing and running rigorous experiments comparing QRNG-enhanced and deterministic AI systems. Experimental design, data collection, analysis, and reporting.

### Theoretical work
Developing and refining conceptual frameworks connecting quantum mechanics, consciousness, and AI alignment. Literature synthesis, philosophical analysis, and formal modeling.

### Fundraising and matchmaking
Grant applications, partnership development, investor outreach, and helping connect people with projects and projects with resources.

### Writing and public relations
Research papers, preprints, blog posts, articles, social media content, podcast appearances, conference talks, wiki pages, tutorials, and guides.

### Community operations
Onboarding new members, moderation, organizing events and weekly calls, managing social media presence, mentoring, and recruiting.

### Review and QA
Code review, peer review of research, testing, and feedback on proposals and documents.

## How to contribute code

1. Fork the relevant repository
2. Create a branch with a descriptive name
3. Make your changes with clear commit messages
4. Open a pull request. In the PR description, explain what the change does and why. Link any related GitHub issues.
5. A maintainer will review your PR. Address feedback and iterate.

For write access to repositories or project boards, ask an admin on Discord or during a weekly call.

## Contribution tracking

> **Under construction.** We are developing a formal contribution tracking system that will record and recognize all forms of work: code, research, writing, community building, fundraising, and more. This system will inform future decisions about authorship, ownership shares, and resource allocation. Details will be published when ready.

For now, if you don't want to contribute without being recognized for it, maybe this community is not for you.

## Research co-authorship

Anyone who contributes meaningfully to a research paper has the right to be included as an author. Authorship order is determined by the person with the largest contribution to that paper. If the ordering is contested, the admins investigate and make a final decision.
